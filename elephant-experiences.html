Elephant Experiences

Time spent with the elephants of Pinnawala

The river, the feeding, the slow walk back through the palms — what a day around the herd actually looks like, and how to be there for the best of it.

Home/Elephant Experiences

The setting

Why people come to Pinnawala

Pinnawala is known across Sri Lanka for its elephants. A large group lives here, cared for by mahouts who have often worked with the same animals for years.

What makes it unusual is the ordinariness of it. The elephants are not performing. They eat, they doze in the shade, they squabble gently over a good branch, and twice a day they make their way down to the Ma Oya to bathe. Visitors watch from the bank, from the road, and — on the quieter paths — from a few metres away with a mahout alongside.

We are a local family based minutes from the village, so we can tell you what the elephants have actually been doing that week rather than what a brochure says they do.

A day around the herd

How the hours usually fall

The rhythm below is what a typical day looks like. Exact times change with the season, the weather and the river, so we confirm the day's plan with you directly.

Early morning — the cool hours

The best light and the fewest people. Elephants are active, the air is still cool, and the jungle is loud with birds. This is when photographs are easiest and the animals are at their most relaxed.

Feeding

Cut palm fronds and branches are brought in by the cartload. Watching an elephant work its way through a pile of foliage — stripping, folding and sweeping it up with the trunk — is far more absorbing than it sounds.

Down to the river

The walk to the Ma Oya and the bathe that follows is the centrepiece of the day. The elephants lie down in the shallows, roll, spray and are scrubbed by their mahouts. It usually lasts a good while, so there is no need to rush.

Afternoon in the shade

Back up to the trees for the hottest part of the day. A quieter time, good for watching the younger animals, and a natural point to break off for lunch and a swim before coming out again later.

Please note. Opening hours, ticketing, age limits and exactly which activities are offered on your dates are confirmed with you when you enquire. We do not promise direct contact with any animal in advance — that decision rests with the mahouts on the day, and depends on the elephant.

What you can expect to see

Scenes from our own visits

Every photograph below was taken on a Pinnawala Elephant Tour outing. They show what visitors have seen — not a guarantee of what will happen on a particular morning.

Feeding at close range

Standing a few metres from an animal that weighs several tonnes while it quietly works through a pile of greenery is the moment most visitors remember.

Younger animals

Calves stay close to the adults and are endlessly busy. They are usually the reason a planned twenty-minute stop turns into an hour.

On the move

The walk between the feeding ground and the river passes under palms and bamboo. Small groups follow at a respectful distance with a mahout leading.

A supervised meeting

Where the mahouts judge it appropriate, visitors may be invited to come closer. Always supervised, never hurried, and always the elephant's choice as much as yours.

In the landscape

Away from the crowds the elephants simply blend into the jungle. Catching one half-hidden in the green is a very different photograph from a zoo shot.

The surrounding country

Coconut groves, paddy and rubber estates run right up to the riverbank. The setting is as much a part of the experience as the animals themselves.

Visiting well

A few things worth knowing

Come early, stay longer

Mornings are cooler, quieter and better lit. If you are staying with us, you can be at the river before the day coaches arrive.

Bring a long lens if you have one

Much of the best watching is from a distance. A zoom or a phone with a decent telephoto will do far more for you than getting closer would.

Follow the mahouts

They know each animal's temperament. If they ask you to step back or stay quiet, there is always a reason — please do as they say.
